# Threat model: real-time sessions on a LAN

What RLMesh's real-time sessions (`rlmesh.realtime`, protocol `rlmesh-realtime-v1`) defend on a home or lab network, and what they don't. **Today** marks behaviour of the current tree, and **0.2** what has to change before the 0.2.0 release. Keep this page in step with the code.

## What is worth protecting

- **Motion.** Who may move a robot: leases, action chunks, arm and disarm, e-stop and its clear.
- **Data.** Camera frames, joint states and recordings.
- **Keys.** The host's identity (`host.key` in its state directory), each device's key, and `local.key`.

## Who is on the network

| Party                         | Holds                                                                       | Can do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Anyone on the LAN             | nothing                                                                     | See Zenoh traffic and its metadata (key expressions, participant names, timing). Read any part that is signed but not sealed. Flood or jam the link. It can't attach: the IK handshake admits only keys in `devices.toml` (plus `local`). Control traffic is encrypted. **Today** the host starts a session for any liveliness token in its tenant, so a stranger can make it spin up sessions. **0.2:** only allowlisted devices may create sessions, up to a cap.                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| A stolen board (ESP32)        | its device key in NVS, the host's public key and address, Wi-Fi credentials | Attach as its device, at its cap (`model` by default), and drive the parts its entry covers, or every part by default (`**`). **Today** it may also attach with any role: as an embodiment it can clear any latched e-stop. **0.2:** each entry caps its roles, and only the robot that latched an e-stop (or `local`) clears it. Fix: `rlmesh devices revoke <id>` ends its sessions at once. **Today** a sketch can also be built with an address ending in `#key` (`kAddress`), and that key is `local`'s, so a board flashed that way can do everything. **0.2:** boards join with their provisioned device key only, and the firmware carries no secret. |

## Streams: signed is not authenticated per sender

A part's stream key `k_stream` is shared by its publisher and every reader. In `sign` mode a sample is MAC'd under that key, so **any reader of a part can forge its samples and its `_applied` entries**. Sealing encrypts too, but the forgery problem stays. **0.2:** use per-publisher Ed25519 signatures on `_applied` and the command paths, or rename the mode and state the limit here. Until then, treat a part's readers as able to write its stream.

## Robots fail safe on their own

An edge holds or goes safe when its lease lapses, its queue runs dry or it loses the host. This needs no message from the network, so cutting a link stops motion rather than freezing the last command. An e-stop latches on the robot, and a reboot keeps the latch (**0.2:** the ESP32 latch persists in NVS).

## Out of scope

- A compromised host machine or `local` account.
- Physical access beyond taking a board: JTAG, flash readout of other secrets.
- Radio-level denial of service.
- The supply chain of the toolchains and crates.
- Confidentiality of anything sent in `sign` mode.
